<h2>Overview</h2>

Oneness, equality, threeness, and hierarchy are the four main concepts affirmed by trinitarianism. Overemphasis of any one of these aspects leads to the nontrinitarian doctrinal position given in italic text under the concept name (e.g. overemphasis of oneness leads to modalism). Notice how concepts in opposite corners are, well, opposites. The doctrinal position in each corner usually amounts to outright denial of the concept at the opposite corner. Furthermore, if you live in one of the corners, you tend to prop up a straw man of trinitarianism in the opposite corner from you. Why? Well, you know trinitarianism is different from your belief, and, as I mentioned a moment ago, since the trinitarians you interact with are always emphasizing the thing in the opposite corner, the thing you deny, you think they must live in that corner all the time. It's an honest mistake.

Let's go through each of these four concepts to see some examples of what I mean.

<h3>Threeness</h3>

Definition: God exists eternally in three distinct persons. By this we do not mean that there are three separate God beings, but that the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are unique identities within the one being or essence of God. The three persons of the Trinity interact with one another and are mentioned together in ways that make it clear we should not think of them as being one person or one identity.
