There is a popular addage in Mormonism that goes like this: "I never said it would be easy; I only said it would be worth it." A friend who served with me in the Kentucky Louisville Mission even had it written into his Bible (in Jacobian English, no less) as Matthew 28:21 -- half-jokingly, of course.

Where did this come from? I heard once that it came from an Especially For Youth (an unofficial LDS youth program) counselor, but who really knows? The important thing to realize is that it simply did not come out of Jesus' mouth.

> Come to Me, all who are weary and heavy-laden, and I will give you rest. Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For My yoke is easy and My burden is light.

Matthew 11:30 NASB

Another important thing to realize is that this is not an issue of Mormon doctrine, but rather of Mormon culture. Do a Google search for "I never said it would be easy" and you'll find several official and unofficial LDS sites that point out this very same dichotomy. Many Mormons realize that this phrase is false doctrine, and yet it continues to come up in LDS sacrament meeting talks, testimonies, and the like. In my opinion, the phrase is a subtle by-product of the LDS works-centered gospel. It _sounds_ enough like Mormon doctrine that people don't stop to check it out before they share it.

In the coming months I'll be comparing the LDS and Evangelical Christian theologies in more depth, but for now it's important only to say that Mormon doctrine and Mormon culture are two different beasts. Evangelical Christians often fail to make a distinction between them, partly due to the fact that actual Mormon doctrine is difficult to define in solid terms.
