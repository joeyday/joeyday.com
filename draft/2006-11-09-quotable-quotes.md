I used to love the quote, "Happiness lies in the journey, not in the destination." It reflects well on the Mormon concept of Eternal Progression, and it just seems wise from the moment you read it. If heaven is a place with pearly gates and golden streets where we all just sit around playing harps, and there's not really much more to do or learn, I didn't want to be there. I wanted to be where the action was, in the learning, the growing, the progressing -- in the journey.

But who coined this phrase? Was it coined by a Latter-day Saint? Was it even coined by someone with a Christian worldview? The more I think about it the more I realize it was probably coined by some motivational speaker who's entire worldview consisted of something along the lines of, "Gee, I wonder how much money I can con out of all the people at the next convention I'm speaking at."

In contrast, the writer of Hebrews declared:

> So then, there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God, for whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works as God did from his. Let us therefore strive to enter that rest, so that no one may fall by the same sort of disobedience.

And St. John says in his Revelation:

> Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away, and the sea was no more. And I saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, â€œBehold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God. He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning nor crying nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away."

Happiness, then, for those with a Biblical Christian worldview, lies in the destination. Anything else is the empty philosophy of the world!
