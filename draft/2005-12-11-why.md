People who ask, "Why do bad things happen to good people?" are asking the wrong question. In fact, I believe there's a great lie of Satan wrapped up in that question: the "good people" part. The Bible is clear that all have sinned ((Rom. 3:23)). There is no one good ((Ps. 14:2-3)) except God ((Mark 10:18)).

If God is the only one good, then asking the question, "Why do bad things happen to good people?" is equal to asking, "Why do bad things happen to God?" and that's an awfully silly question, as I think everyone would agree that nothing bad has ever happened to God. The fact is, whatever "bad things" you have witnessed happening to someone you might have thought was a "good person" were really deserved by that person.

Now, don't hear me wrong here. I'm not saying your friend is a worse sinner than other people. The reality here is that you and I and everyone else you know deserved to have those same bad things happen to us, but we were, for whatever reason, spared. You see, that's the question we should be asking ourselves: "Why don't bad things happen to all bad people?" The answer, of course, is that God sometimes extends mercy and grace. Just because God extends mercy sometimes doesn't mean he is obligated to extend it all the time. Remember, we all deserve the bad things that are happening to us. God is just in letting them happen to us, and we should be extremely grateful for the times we are gracefully spared.
