This may seem funny to you at first, but for the longest time now I've been looking for a replacement for the church magazines I grew up reading. In case you have no idea what I'm talking about, the LDS Church publishes three high-quality <a href="http://lds.org/ldsorg/v/index.jsp?vgnextoid=3a4d3f7719605010VgnVCM1000004e94610aRCRD&locale=0">magazines</a>, <cite>Friend</cite> for children, <cite>New Era</cite> for youth, and <cite>Ensign</cite> for adults ((The church also publishes a fourth magazine called <cite>Liahona</cite>, which is an international publication printed in fifty different languages. I didn't mention it above because it contains generally the same content as the <cite>Ensign</cite>, with occasional articles from <cite>New Era</cite> and <cite>Friend</cite>. English is one of the fifty languages, but English readers don't generally subscribe since they can just get the other three magazines.)). They are a staple in Mormon homes and I've honestly missed them since leaving the church.

<!--more-->

<h4><cite>Christianity Today</cite></h4>

<img src='http://joeyday.com/wp-content/uploads/2007/12/christianity-today.jpg' alt='Christianity Today Magazine' class='lpic' />

The first year Janene and I were married, we got a subscription to <cite><a href="http://www.christianitytoday.com/ct/">Christianity Today</a></cite>. It's an all right magazine, and I did enjoy it while we were subscribed, but it's overly commercialized for my taste and at times seemed a bit directionless in a theological sense. In other words, it seemed to take submissions from too many varied sources without a whole lot of doctrinal discernment.

On top of these concerns, we had a bad experience when we canceled our subscription after the first year. We sent back the little renewal card with the "no" box clearly checked, but then they sent us what appeared to be a collections notice. We hadn't received any issues we hadn't paid for, and knew we owed them absolutely no money. When I called the number and went through several layers of touch-tone menus, I finally reached an option that said something like, "if you meant to unsubscribe, press x." When I heard that, it was obvious to me this was an absolutely fake collections notice. It really left a bad taste in our mouth. We will almost certainly never subscribe to that magazine again, and we were really hesitant to subscribe to any other so-called "Christian" magazines.

Recently, however, a good friend ordered me two gift subscriptions that have restored my faith in Christian magazines: Michael Horton's <cite><a href="http://www.modernreformation.org">Modern Reformation</a></cite> and R.C. Sproul's (Ligonier Ministries') <cite><a href="http://www.ligonier.org/tt.php">Tabletalk</a></cite>.
