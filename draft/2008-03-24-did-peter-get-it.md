I mentioned to an LDS friend today in passing that I don't think Peter really "got it" until after the resurrectionâ€”he didn't really know what Jesus had come to do. Sure, Peter was confident Jesus was the Messiah, but I don't think he had much of a clue what that meant until Jesus rose from the dead. My comment was somewhat tangential to the conversation we were having, and I assumed my friend would agree with me, but he quickly stopped me to ask what I meant. Unfortunately, we ended our conversation shortly afterward and I don't feel we discussed it thoroughly enough. I wanted to explain more what I meant and was hoping to hear more from my friend as to why he disagreed, so I hope he's reading and willing to post a comment.

There is a BYU devotional speech that was delivered by Spencer W. Kimball titled, "Peter, My Brother". In it, Kimball attempts to debunk the notion that Peter had any sort of fall from grace. He stops short of saying that Peter was sinless, but in my opinion he really puts Peter on a high pedastal. He explains away Peter's thrice-repeated denial by postulating that, instead of a prophecy, Christ's statement that Peter would deny him was actually a commandment to do so. And so, Peter's denial was not sinful, but actually in compliance with Jesus' wishes. I wonder if Kimball would say the same about Jesus' prophecy that Judas would betray him? Was Judas simply following orders?
