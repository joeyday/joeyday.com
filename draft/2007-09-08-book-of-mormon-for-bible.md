I'm not exactly sure how interested anyone else will be, but I've converted the Book of Mormon to a format that is compatible with <a href="http://palmbibleplus.sourceforge.net">Palm Bible+</a>, an open source Bible reading program for Palm OS devices. I also plan to release the other LDS Standard Works (i.e. Doctrine & Covenants and the Pearl of Great Price) but I'm not sure when I'll really get around to formatting them.

To download the Book of Mormon for Bible+, click below.

- <a href="http://downloads.joeyday.com/bofm.pdb" class="download">bofm.pdb</a>

To download Palm Bible+ and other Bible modules including the KJV, visit <a href="http://palmbibleplus.sourceforge.net">palmbibleplus.sourceforge.net</a>.

The rest of this post contains some background on why I decided to create this file and make it available.

<h4>Laridian MyBible</h4>

I love having the Bible on my Palm PDA. I love being able to read it whenever I want to and wherever I happen to be. For Palm Bible software, I don't think there's anything better than <a href="http://www.laridiansales.com/order/productpages/3MBPGM003.asp?ref=apwwrnvnh&order_platform=palm&palmoslevel=205">MyBible 4</a> from <a href="http://www.laridian.com/?ref=apwwrnvnh">Laridian Electronic Publishing</a>. I carry over a dozen popular translations of the Bible plus five or six popular Bible commentaries on my Palm. I also have Laridian's <a href="http://www.laridian.com/palm/infodailyreader.asp?ref=apwwrnvnh">DailyReader</a>, which not only lets me read popular devotionals (I'm currently making my way through Piper's <cite><a href="http://www.amazon.com/o/asin/1576738396/joeyday">A Godward Life</a></cite>) but also chunks out the Bible into daily sections for personal reading.

The big features for me when it comes to Bible software are a fast search engine that's easy to use and a quick way to jump straight to a specified chapter or verse. MyBible does both of these things very well, and seems to me more polished than some of its competitors like Olive Tree Bible Reader. Another issue, of course, is price. I should mention that MyBible isn't free. However, I do think it's competitively priced and well worth the money for what it does.

One gripe I have is that MyBible isn't very flexible when it comes to content other than English Bibles. For instance, I also enjoy having the Hebrew Old Testament and Greek New Testament (though I can't really read them), the Qur'an, and the LDS Standard Works (i.e. Book of Mormon, Doctrine & Covenants, and Pearl of Great Price) on my Palm. None of these titles are available for MyBible, so I've had to turn to additional software. I should say that of these, I would expect MyBible to support the original languages of the Bible but not necessarily the Qur'an or LDS Standard Works. After all, their mission statement as a Christian software company is to make Bible reading software, and as I've already said, I think they're the best at what they do.

<h4>MarkMyScriptures and HandyScriptures</h4>

For the LDS Standard Works, there are really only two programs worth using: <a href="http://www.skimware.com/">MarkMyScriptures</a> and <a href="http://www.handyscriptures.org/">Handy Scriptures</a>. MarkMyScriptures isn't free. I did try the trial once and can't remember now if I didn't purchase it simply because I was too cheap to spend the money or because I found serious flaws in the program. I tend to suspect the latter, but wouldn't be surprised if it was simply the former. At any rate, since it's been so long since I've used the program I'll hold any comments.

Handy Scriptures is free, and up until now has been my LDS scripture program of choice. However, I do have a major complaint with Handy Scriptures regarding its navigation method. To navigate to a passage you open up a little dialog box and type in the name of the book followed by the chapter and verse. It does auto-complete the book name, which makes things slightly more convenient, but not by much. Most other Bible software I've used pops up a grid with all the book names so you can choose the book you want with a single tap. In Handy Scriptures it can take up to four letters to choose the book you want. This is made worse by having to type the complete reference every time you want to flip to a different verse. In most other Bible software, you can simply type the verse number to jump to that verse in the current chapter or type the chapter and verse to jump to a passage within the same book. Additionally, the search engine in Handy Scriptures is okay but again not as fast or easy to use as MyBible.

I should mention that the LDS Stardard Works are available for all the popular eBook readers for Palm OS. However, since the eBook readers don't really give you any way to jump to specific passages, they're essentially useless for scripture reading.

<h4>Palm Bible+</h4>

Another great Palm Bible program, Bible+, is open source and is a close runner up to MyBible in terms of features. Its chapter and verse navigation is identical to MyBible's and its search engine is only slightly behind MyBible's in terms of speed and ease of use. I like Bible+ and am a big supporter of open source software. I would use it as my main Bible reading software if the interface were slightly more user friendly and had more English Bibles available.

The thing I like about Bible+ is that it's not limited to the usual 66 books of the Bible. The developers have opened it up so you can specify custom book names. Not only will you find versions of the bible that include the Apocrypha, you'll also find versions of the Qur'an and Dead Sea Scrolls readily available. Additionally, Bible+ supports languages that use non-Latin alphabets. Not only will you find versions of the bible in Japanese and Chinese, you'll also find the original Greek and Hebrew, the Latin Vulgate, and even the original Arabic Qur'an.  In this repect, Bible+ seems to suffer from the exact opposite of the problem Laridian has: it has phenomenal support for languages other than English and scriptures other than the Bible, but has poor English Bible support because there's no cohesive leadership or significant funding behind the project to help it secure the rights to use many of the popular modern Bible translations. Given the strengths and limitations of MyBible and Bible+ and the ways in which they complement one another, I'm happy to use them both together on my Palm.

<h4>LDS Standard works for Bible+</h4>

So, that brings us back around to the topic at hand. I've finally decided to stop putting up with Handy Scriptures' poor navigation and convert the Book of Mormon and eventually the entire LDS Standard Works to Bible+ format.
