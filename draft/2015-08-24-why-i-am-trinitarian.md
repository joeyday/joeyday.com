I don't typically do this, but I think Kimmons's article is fairly representative of the typical apologetic for the Mormon view of God over and against the Trinity, so today I want to interact with his argument and try to demonstrate some of its flaws.

<h2>The straw man</h2>

<blockquote>God is everywhere and nowhere, everything and nothing. God is three people, and yet one person, and yet not a person at all. Even though God cannot be understood, we understand Him. Even though God cannot be defined, we will define Him, and if you have a definition that is different from ours, you obviously believe in a different God and are therefore a heretic and a villain. God cannot be known, and only by knowing God can we be saved.</blockquote>

This is how Kimmons defines the Trinity at the outset of his article. He follows that with a pointed question, "Is this the language of a rational person?" No, it's not, and furthermore it's not the language of trinitarianism.
